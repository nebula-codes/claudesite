Sunlight Zone

0 - 200 meters

The brightest part of the ocean, where photosynthesis occurs. Warm, filled with life, and the only zone where plants can grow.

Dolphin

Intelligent marine mammals that use echolocation to navigate and hunt.

Sea Turtle

Ancient reptiles that have roamed oceans for over 100 million years.

Jellyfish

Gelatinous creatures that have existed for 500 million years, predating dinosaurs.

Twilight Zone

200 - 1,000 meters

Dim light barely penetrates here. The largest animal migration on Earth occurs daily as creatures rise to feed at night and descend during the day.

Hatchetfish

Thin, silvery fish with bioluminescent organs on their bellies to match downwelling light.

Lanternfish

The most abundant vertebrate on Earth. Their bioluminescence creates "false moonlight".

Swordfish

Powerful predators with special organs that heat their eyes and brain for better hunting.

Midnight Zone

1,000 - 4,000 meters

Complete darkness. Temperatures near freezing. Bioluminescence is the only light. Creatures here have bizarre adaptations to survive in this alien world.

Anglerfish

Females have a bioluminescent lure dangling from their heads. Males are tiny parasites that fuse to females.

Giant Squid

Legends of the Kraken. Eyes the size of dinner plates. Can grow up to 43 feet long.

Vampire Squid

Not actually a squid. Has bioluminescent organs and can turn itself "inside out" when threatened.

Abyssal Zone

4,000 - 6,000 meters

The abyss. Crushing pressure of 400 atmospheres. No light has ever reached here. Life moves slowly, conserving energy in this food-scarce environment.

Gulper Eel

A mouth bigger than its body. Can swallow prey larger than itself. Glows pink when disturbed.

Tripod Fish

Stands on three elongated fins on the seafloor, facing into current to catch drifting food.

Sea Pig

A type of sea cucumber. Walks on leg-like tube feet, filtering nutrients from mud.

Hadal Zone

6,000 - 11,000 meters

Named after Hades, the Greek underworld. The deepest trenches on Earth. Pressure exceeds 1,000 atmospheres. More people have been to the Moon than here.

Mariana Snailfish

Deepest living fish ever recorded at 8,178 meters. Translucent with no scales.

Amphipods

Shrimp-like creatures. Some species in the Mariana Trench grow to enormous sizes.

Xenophyophores

Giant single-celled organisms. The largest living cells on Earth, up to 20cm across.

The Mariana Trench

The deepest point on Earth: 10,994 meters (36,070 feet)

If Mount Everest were placed in the trench, its peak would still be over 2km underwater.

First reached by humans in 1960 (Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh in the Trieste).

Only four dives to the bottom have ever been made.

Mysterious Ocean Sounds

The Bloop (1997)

An ultra-low frequency sound detected by underwater microphones. Loud enough to be heard 5,000km away. Initially thought to be from an unknown massive creature, later identified as likely from icequakes.

Julia (1999)

A sound that lasted 15 seconds, resembling whale songs but far too loud. Origin still debated.

The Upsweep

A continuous sound detected since 1991. Seasonal variations suggest volcanic or seismic activity.
